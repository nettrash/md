md is a Markdown editor and live previewer for iPhone and iPad. Write plain Markdown, watch the finished page take shape as you type, then print it, export it as a PDF, or turn a folder of chapters into a whole book.

WRITE AND SEE
• Edit and Preview on iPhone; on iPad, a Split view puts the source and the rendered page side by side — and the two panes scroll as one, so the passage you are reading is the paragraph you are writing.
• The preview is the printed page: one renderer draws the preview, the printout and the PDF — the same layout, set for paper (white pages, dark ink, print-sized type).
• Headings, lists, task lists, tables, quotes, code blocks, links and images — plus LaTeX math, Mermaid graphs and PlantUML diagrams, all drawn on your device by engines built into the app.
• Contents lists every heading and jumps the editor and the preview to it.
• Private author notes — an HTML comment on its own line whose text begins with “note:” — are listed in the Notes menu and never appear in the preview, the PDF or the printout.
• A live word and character count sits under every page.
• Undo and redo, always within reach.

PRINT AND EXPORT
• Print or export real A4 pages. Breaks fall between lines, never through them, and \newpage starts a fresh page wherever you decide.
• Share the rendered PDF, share the Markdown source, or save the PDF into Files.

WRITER MODE: BOOKS
• A book is simply a folder: its subfolders are chapters, its Markdown files are articles, ordered by name. Start one from the opening screen — New Book — or open a folder you already have.
• The book lists your chapters and articles; tap one to write, and tap another to move on. Long-press or swipe a row to rename, reorder or delete it. The order is written back to the file names, so it stays true in Files.
• Compile the whole book into one PDF, or export it as a standard EPUB 3 with a proper table of contents — math and diagrams travel with it as images.

FILES YOU CAN KEEP
• Your documents are ordinary .md, .markdown and .txt files, kept where you choose — on your device or in your own iCloud Drive — and saved in the encoding they arrived in.
• Built-in examples, including a small sample book, show everything the app can do.

PRIVATE BY DESIGN
• No accounts, no sign-in, no analytics, no tracking, no ads.
• Math and diagrams are rendered on your device. The only thing md fetches from the network is an image your own document points at by URL.

Written in Swift and SwiftUI, with no accounts and no servers. The only outside code in the app is the open-source engines that draw the math and the diagrams — KaTeX, Mermaid, Graphviz and PlantUML — bundled in, and run on your device. The source code is public.
