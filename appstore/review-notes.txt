md is a Markdown editor and live previewer for iPhone and iPad. This update (1.6) adds formatting commands (a bar above the iPhone keyboard, a Format menu on iPad), a Quick Look extension that previews TextBundles, and EPUB export that takes author, language, series and cover from a document's front matter.

1. ACCESS — NOTHING TO SET UP
No account, sign-in, registration, in-app purchase or subscription. Every feature works at launch, so no demo credentials exist or are needed.

No test file is needed: the app ships with sample documents. Open any document, tap the Examples button in the toolbar (on iPhone, in its three-dot More menu) and pick one (Welcome, Formatting, Tables, Code, Images, Math, Diagrams, Writer Tools).

2. A THREE-MINUTE WALKTHROUGH OF WHAT IS NEW
a) Tap New Document on the opening screen and type a few words, then select one. On iPhone, the bar above the keyboard formats it: Bold, Italic, Code, Link, Heading, the lists, Quote, Outdent and Indent. On iPad, the same commands are in the toolbar's Format menu. Tapping Bold again takes the bold off. With a hardware keyboard: Command-B, Command-I, Option-Command-1 for a heading.
b) With text selected, type * or _ and the selection is wrapped rather than replaced. A switch in the toolbar's Typing menu turns this off.
c) Open the Formatting example, tap Share and export a TextBundle to Files. In the Files app, long-press it and choose Quick Look: the preview is md's rendered page.
d) Add lines such as "author: A. Writer" and "language: fr" between two "---" lines at the top of a document, then Share, Export as EPUB: the book's details carry them.
e) Unchanged: Find (Command-F), the Examples menu (Math, Diagrams, Code, Example Book for writer mode) and the Share button's exports to PDF, HTML, EPUB, LaTeX, TextBundle and SVG, each saved where you pick in Files.

The app creates no hidden files or sidecar metadata; a book folder's access is kept as a security-scoped bookmark.

3. PURPOSE AND AUDIENCE
Writers, developers and students who keep notes, documentation or a book in Markdown and want to see, print and export the page. Nothing is shared, published or hosted, so there is nothing to moderate, report or block.

4. NETWORK AND EXTERNAL SERVICES
The app contacts no servers of ours; there are none. No analytics, advertising or tracking, and no data collected (Privacy Nutrition Label: "Data Not Collected").

The app fetches only what the user's own document names by URL: an image it references, so it can be shown and printed, and, when an EPUB is exported with a front-matter "cover:" https URL, that one image (at most 20 MB in 15 seconds, at most five redirects, https only). Nothing else is ever sent or fetched. The Quick Look extension blocks every remote load: a previewed document's remote images are not fetched.

5. NO DOWNLOADED CODE (2.5.2)
Math, chemistry, diagrams and code highlighting are drawn by open-source JavaScript engines bundled in the app and served to WKWebView from a private, offline scheme: KaTeX (with its mhchem extension), Mermaid, Graphviz, PlantUML, and highlight.js. No executable code is downloaded or updated at runtime. There are no third-party Swift packages or SDKs.

6. PERMISSIONS
None are requested: no camera, microphone, location, contacts, calendars, notifications, or tracking prompt. Files are reached only through the system document browser and folder picker — the user always chooses what the app may touch.

7. REGIONAL DIFFERENCES, REGULATED CONTENT
None. The app behaves identically everywhere, contains no regulated or age-restricted content, and includes no third-party trademarked material beyond the open-source engines named above, each bundled under its own open-source licence.

Encryption: ITSAppUsesNonExemptEncryption is false — the app implements no encryption.

Minimum system: iOS and iPadOS 26. The source code and the privacy policy are public.
